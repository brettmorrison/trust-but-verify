# A grandchild in trouble

## The call

The phone rings late, or very early. A young voice, crying, muffled:

> "Grandma? I'm in trouble. Please don't tell Mom."

You say a name — because you're frightened and trying to help. They take it.
*"Yes, it's me."* That's the only piece of information the scheme needed and you
gave it in the first four seconds, which is exactly how it was designed.

Then a second voice: a lawyer, a public defender, a bail bondsman, a police
sergeant. There's been an accident. A DUI. An arrest in another state. Bail must
be posted in the next two hours or the arraignment is missed and it becomes a
weekend in jail.

And a gag order. There always is. *"The judge has sealed this."* *"Don't tell his
mother, he's so ashamed."*

Then a courier comes to your house for cash. Or you're walked through a wire, or
a Bitcoin machine, or gift cards.

## Why it works on people who are not naive

Because it isn't aimed at your judgment. It's aimed under it.

A grandchild in danger produces a physical response before any thinking happens.
Your body dumps adrenaline, and for several minutes the careful, checking part of
your mind is running at a fraction of its normal speed. That's biology, not
gullibility.

Then they add secrecy — the thing that stops you from making the one call that
would end it — and a clock, so there's no room to breathe.

They also know things. Names, a school, a recent trip. All of it is on Facebook,
in an obituary, in a graduation announcement, or bought from a data broker for
pennies. See [how they got your information](/how-they-got-your-information).

And increasingly, the voice is genuinely his. See [voice
cloning](/scams/voice-cloning) — three seconds of audio from any video anywhere
is enough now.

## What to do — the four moves

### 1. Hang up

You will not be able to think while they are talking. That's not weakness; it's
the intended effect. Hang up first, think second.

### 2. Call your grandchild yourself

On the number you've always had for him. Not the number that called. Not any
number they gave you.

### 3. If he doesn't answer, call his parents

Immediately. Do not honor the secrecy request. If it is real, his mother needs to
know right now anyway — no genuine emergency is improved by his grandmother
keeping it secret.

**The secrecy request is the proof.** Real families call each other in a crisis.
Only scams need the family kept apart.

### 4. Ask for the code word

If your family has one, use it. If you don't have one, [set one this
week](/the-three-steps#the-family-code-word) — a meal, four minutes, everyone
present.

If you don't have a code word, ask something no stranger could know and no
computer could find. Not his birthday, not his middle name, not his dog. Ask what
you two ate the last time he visited. Ask what you always call his father.

A scammer will get angry, or make an excuse — *"he's too upset to talk"* — or
hang up. Any of those is your answer.

## Things that are always true

- **Bail is never paid in gift cards.** Not once, anywhere, ever.
- **No court, jail, or lawyer sends a courier to your home for cash.** If someone
  is coming to your door, call the police.
- **No judge orders a grandmother not to call her own family.** Gag orders do not
  work that way and do not apply to you.
- **Real lawyers give you a firm name, a bar number, and a callback line** and
  they do not object when you look it up.

---

## Prevention, this week

**Set the code word.** It's the single highest-value four minutes available to
your family. One word, shared out loud, never posted anywhere.

**Tighten up what's public.** Set family social media to friends-only. Graduation
posts, vacation photos, and "so proud of my grandson at [school]" are raw
material.

**Talk about it before it happens.** A grandmother who has heard this described
once will recognize it mid-call. That recognition is the whole defense.

## What loving your family actually looks like here

Making that second phone call isn't doubting your grandson. It's the most
protective thing you can do, and if it's ever real, it's how the rest of the
family finds out in time to help.

Ninety seconds and one callback. That's what stands between this scheme and every
family that knows about it.
