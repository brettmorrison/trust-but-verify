# The 5-minute lobby talk

Anyone can deliver this. No slides, no expertise, no preparation beyond reading
it once. Works at a residents' meeting, a church group, a library, a senior
center, or a family dinner.

**Bracketed text is direction, not to be read aloud.**

---

## [0:00] Open with a story, not a statistic

> Last month a woman in a building not far from here got a call. It was her
> grandson — crying, saying he'd been arrested and please don't tell his mother.
>
> She had eleven hundred dollars in a courier's hands within the hour.
>
> Her grandson was at work the whole time. He never knew any of it happened until
> she told him that night.

*[If you have a real local story, use that instead. Local beats dramatic every
time. Never name anyone.]*

---

## [0:45] Say the part that lets people relax

> I want to say something before I say anything else.
>
> That woman is not foolish. She raised four children and ran a business for
> thirty years.
>
> The people who called her do this full time. They work in call centers. They
> have scripts, supervisors, and quotas. They practice.
>
> So nobody in this room is going to out-think a professional, and I'm not going
> to ask you to. I'm going to ask you to be *slower* than one. That, you can do
> every single time.

---

## [1:30] The three steps — the core of the whole talk

> Here's the whole thing. Three steps, before any money moves.
>
> **One. Look up the number yourself.** Not the number they gave you. Not the
> number in the text. Not the number on the popup. The number on the back of your
> card, or on your statement.
>
> **Two. Call the person yourself.** Hang up first, then call. If it's really your
> bank, they'll still be there. If it's really your grandson, he'll answer.
>
> **Three. Wait a day.** Nothing legitimate in your life needs money in the next
> twenty minutes. Not the IRS. Not Medicare. Not your bank. Every one of those
> will still exist tomorrow morning.

*[Say all three again, slowly. Repetition is the entire delivery method.]*

---

## [2:45] The three signs

> How do you know when to use those three steps? Three signs.
>
> **It came to you.** You didn't start this. They called you.
>
> **It moved you fast.** Fear, or urgency, or worry about someone you love. If
> your chest went tight — that's the sign.
>
> **It wants a transfer.** Money, or a code, or somebody wanting into your
> computer.
>
> If all three are there, treat it as a scam until it's proven otherwise. And it
> can only be proven on a number you looked up yourself.

---

## [3:30] The two absolutes

> Two things I want you to remember for the rest of your life.
>
> **Nobody legitimate is ever paid in gift cards.** Not the IRS, not Microsoft,
> not a bail bondsman, not the electric company. If gift cards come up, that's
> the end of the conversation. Every time. No exceptions.
>
> **Your bank will never ask you to move money out of your bank.** Not to a safe
> account, not to protect it, not while they investigate. That request is always
> a scam.

---

## [4:15] The code word

> One last thing, and it's the easiest thing on this list.
>
> There's software now that copies a person's voice from a few seconds of a video.
> It's free. It takes a minute. So "it sounded just like him" is no longer proof
> of anything.
>
> Here's what beats it. Pick a family code word. A street you lived on, a dog's
> name — something that was never posted anywhere.
>
> Tell your children and your grandchildren. Then, when a frightened voice calls,
> you ask for the word.
>
> Four minutes at a Sunday dinner, and it defeats the whole thing.

---

## [4:45] Close on capability, never on fear

> So: look up the number, call them yourself, wait a day.
>
> You don't have to get suspicious of everybody. You just add one step.
>
> And one more thing. If something has already happened to you, or to somebody
> you care about — there's no shame in it, and it's not too late. There's a free
> hotline: 833-372-8311. They'll walk you through it.
>
> I've got sheets here. Take two — one for you and one for a neighbor.

---

## Delivery notes

- **Hand the sheets over personally** as people leave. Don't leave a stack on a
  table.
- **Never ask who in the room has been scammed.** Not even for a show of hands.
  Not even anonymously.
- **Include yourself in the vulnerable group.** "I nearly fell for a text about a
  package last week." It's the permission slip that lets people ask questions.
- **Leave time for questions if you have it.** The questions are where the real
  education happens, and someone will describe something that happened to them
  this week.
- **If someone tells you they've lost money**, in front of others or after: your
  first words are *"I'm glad you told me."* Then, quietly, offer to sit with them
  while they call their bank.
