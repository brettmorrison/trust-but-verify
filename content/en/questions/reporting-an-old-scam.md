# Is it too late to report a scam from months ago?

**No. Report it.**

The money is probably not coming back, recovery mostly depends on hours, not
months. But recovery was never the only reason to report, and several of the
other reasons still apply fully.

---

## What a late report still does

**It builds the case.** Fraud prosecutions are assembled from patterns. Your
report may be the one that ties a phone number to fifty others, or that
establishes how long an operation ran. Investigators regularly work from reports
filed long after the fact.

**It puts you on the list for restitution.** When a case ends in conviction or an
FTC action, money sometimes gets distributed to victims, years later, usually a
fraction. You can only receive it if you're on the record. People miss out on
real money by having never filed.

**It protects other people.** Reports feed warnings, and warnings reach someone's
mother next month.

**It gives you a report number.** Useful for banks, insurers, and sometimes for
taxes.

---

## Where to report

| Where | For what |
|---|---|
| **ic3.gov** | Anything involving the internet, a phone, or a wire |
| **reportfraud.ftc.gov** | All fraud, any age |
| **identitytheft.gov** | If personal information was taken |
| **Local police** | For a report number |
| **833-372-8311** | National Elder Fraud Hotline, they'll help you file. Weekdays, 10 a.m.-6 p.m. Eastern |

Say the date as best you remember it. Approximate is fine. Nobody will criticize
you for the delay, the people taking these reports know exactly why people wait.

---

## Things still worth doing today

Even months later, these all still help:

- **Freeze your credit** at all three bureaus. Free, reversible, and it blocks
  the most common long-tail damage.
- **Check your credit report** at annualcreditreport.com for accounts you didn't
  open.
- **Change passwords**, starting with email, especially any you reused.
- **Watch for the second approach.** Your name is on a list now, and someone will
  call about recovering your money. [That's the next scam.](/scams/recovery-scam)

---

## On the delay itself

Most people wait, and most people wait for the same reason: embarrassment.

Which is worth naming plainly. Shame is the reason this crime is so
under-reported, and under-reporting is the reason it keeps growing. The FBI
believes reported losses are a fraction of the real total.

So filing months late isn't a failure. It's the part where you stop carrying it
alone and put it into a system that counts it.

**Nobody who reads your report will think less of you. They read hundreds a
week.**

## Filing it changes something

Not necessarily your bank balance. But it moves this out of the category of a
private thing you carry, and into the category of a crime somebody is counting.

Investigators build cases from reports filed months and years late. Yours might
be the one that matters, and either way, you'll have set it down.
