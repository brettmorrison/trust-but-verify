# The call that says someone's been kidnapped

## The call

Your phone rings from a number you don't know. The moment you answer, there's
screaming — a young voice, crying, terrified.

Then a different voice, flat and controlled: *"We have your daughter. If you
call anyone — the police, her phone, anyone — we hurt her. You have twenty
minutes to send money or you will never see her again."*

They may know her name. They may know where she goes to school, or that she's
traveling right now. They tell you to stay on the line. They tell you not to
hang up, not even for a second, while you find a wire service or buy gift
cards.

This is different from the [grandparent scam](/scams/grandparent-scam). Nobody
is claiming to be your grandchild in trouble with the law. They are claiming
someone you love has been physically taken, right now, by force — and that
claim, on purpose, is designed to be too terrifying to verify.

## What's actually happening

**Nobody has been kidnapped.** In the FBI's own accounting of these calls,
they are unaware of a single confirmed case where the "victim" described on
the phone was actually kidnapped.

The screaming is not your family member. Sometimes it's a recording. Sometimes
it's a stranger, coached. Increasingly, [it's an AI clone of a real
voice](/scams/voice-cloning) — a few seconds of audio from a video anywhere
online is enough to build one now.

The details that make it convincing — a name, a school, a trip — come from the
same places every scam gets them: social media, an away-from-home post, a
school website. See [how they got your
information](/how-they-got-your-information).

The twenty-minute clock and "don't hang up" instruction exist for one reason:
to make sure you never do the one thing that ends this instantly.

## What to do — right now, while they're still talking

**You can do this without hanging up, if you're too frightened to hang up
first.** Ask someone else in the room to do it for you.

### 1. Get a second phone

Not the one they're on. A different line — another cell, a landline, a
neighbor's phone.

### 2. Call or text the person they say is kidnapped

Directly, on the number you already have for them. Most of the time, they
answer within seconds, confused, completely safe, having no idea any of this
is happening.

### 3. If they don't answer immediately, that is not proof of anything

People miss calls. They're in a meeting, on a plane, asleep, at a movie. Try
again. Text. Call a friend who's with them. Call their workplace or school.
There are a dozen ways to locate a person in the twenty minutes a scammer says
you have — and every one of those minutes is time they were counting on you
not to use.

### 4. Call the police on a separate line

Tell them exactly what's happening. This is a known, named scam pattern.
Officers can often confirm within minutes that the person described is safe,
sometimes while you're still on the phone with them.

**Do not send money while any of this is unconfirmed — and don't feel you
have to stay on the line with the caller while you check.** The line will
still be there. So will your family member, almost certainly already fine.

---

## Things that are always true

- **Real kidnappers do not ask for gift cards or a wire transfer in twenty
  minutes.** Actual ransom demands, on the rare occasion they happen, do not
  look or move like this.
- **A person who is genuinely unreachable for a few minutes has not been
  kidnapped.** Most "confirmations" collapse the moment you try the second
  phone.
- **The instruction to stay on the line is the tell.** No real emergency
  requires you to remain on a call with the person threatening you.

---

## Prevention, this week

**Agree on a family check-in habit.** Not a secret password necessarily — just
an understanding that if anyone gets a call like this, the first move is
always a second line, not the wire counter.

**Set the [family code word](/the-three-steps#the-family-code-word)** the same
way you would for the grandparent scam. It defeats this one too.

## You would handle this

Reading this before it happens is most of the work. The whole scheme depends
on the twenty minutes being the first time you've ever thought about it. It
isn't, now.
