# "You've won" a prize you never entered

Americans over 60 reported **$138 million** in lottery, sweepstakes, and
inheritance scam losses to the FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center in
2025 — up 57% from the year before.

---

## What it sounds like

> "Congratulations! You've been selected as a winner in the Publishers
> Clearing House sweepstakes — $2.5 million and a new Mercedes. Before we can
> release the funds, there's a one-time processing fee and taxes that need to
> be paid first."

Sometimes it's a call. Sometimes a letter that looks official, with a real
sweepstakes company's name on it. Sometimes a check arrives first — for a
few thousand dollars — with instructions to deposit it and wire back a
portion for "taxes" or "fees." The check is fake and will bounce days later,
after the wire is already gone.

**What they want:** money up front, framed as a tax, a fee, or a customs
charge that has to be paid before the "winnings" can be released. Sometimes
they want your bank account number to "deposit" the prize directly.

**The truth:** you cannot win a contest you never entered. And no legitimate
prize, lottery, or sweepstakes — including real ones like Publishers
Clearing House — ever requires you to pay anything first. If you have to pay
to receive it, it isn't a prize.

---

## The tell that ends the conversation

**Any request for payment before a prize arrives is the entire scam.** Not a
detail to weigh — the whole thing. Real winnings arrive, and any taxes owed
on them are handled the same way as with any income: paid to the government
directly, afterward, never to the person handing you a check.

---

## What to do

**Don't pay anything, and don't deposit an unexpected check.** If a bank
lets you deposit a fake check, you're still responsible for the money once
it bounces — sometimes days later, after you've already sent your share of
it away.

**Don't give your bank account number to "deposit your winnings."** A real
sweepstakes doesn't need it, and this is often just a way to get you to say
the number out loud.

**Verify it yourself.** If a call claims to be from a real company, look up
that company's number independently and call them to ask. Publishers
Clearing House, for instance, confirms plainly that it never asks winners to
pay anything.

**Report it** at [reportfraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov) or
[ic3.gov](https://ic3.gov).

---

There is no version of winning that costs you money first. If a prize
requires a payment, a fee, or your bank details before it arrives, the
prize was never real — hang up, and nothing was lost.
