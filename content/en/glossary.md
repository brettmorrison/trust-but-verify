# Plain-word glossary

Nobody should have to learn jargon to keep their savings. But these words come up,
so here they are without the fog.

---

**Two-factor authentication (2FA)** — A second step when logging in, usually a
six-digit code texted to your phone. It's a good thing and you should turn it on.
**Nobody should ever ask you to read that code out loud.** It exists to keep other
people out.

**Verification code / one-time code** — Same thing. The six digits. Say them to no
one, ever, under any circumstances.

**Phishing** — A message pretending to be from a company, designed to get your
password or your call.

**Smishing** — Phishing by text message. You don't need the word; you just need to
never tap a link in a message you didn't ask for.

**Spoofing** — Faking what appears on a screen: a caller ID, a sender's name, an
email address. Cheap and easy. See [can caller ID be faked](/questions/can-caller-id-be-faked).

**Remote access** — Letting someone control your computer from elsewhere. Real
technicians use it. So do scammers, with the same tools: AnyDesk, TeamViewer,
LogMeIn, UltraViewer. **Nobody legitimate calls you and asks for it.**

**Malware** — Harmful software. Real, but far rarer than the popups claiming you
have it.

**Ransomware** — Software that locks your files and demands payment. Mostly aimed
at businesses.

**Data broker** — A company that compiles and sells files about individuals.
Legal. The reason a stranger knows your address.

**Data breach** — When a company you dealt with gets hacked and your details
escape. Not your fault and not preventable by you.

**Sucker list** — A list of people who've been scammed before, sold to other
scammers. The reason the [second scam](/scams/recovery-scam) always comes.

**Cryptocurrency / Bitcoin** — Digital money that moves like cash and cannot be
reversed. Not itself a scam, but the favorite vehicle, for exactly that reason.

**Crypto ATM / Bitcoin kiosk** — The machines in gas stations. **If anyone is on
the phone with you while you stand at one, you are being robbed.** There is no
other reason for that call.

**Wire transfer** — Bank-to-bank money movement, usually irreversible, sometimes
recallable within hours if you call immediately.

**Zelle / Venmo / Cash App** — Instant payment apps. Built for people who already
trust each other. Money sent is generally gone.

**Chargeback** — Disputing a credit card charge. The strongest consumer
protection available to an ordinary person. It's why credit cards are the safest
way to pay someone you're unsure of.

**Credit freeze** — Blocking new credit accounts from being opened in your name.
Free, reversible, doesn't affect your score or your existing cards. The single
best hour of protection available.

**Trusted contact** — Someone you name at your bank or brokerage who can be
called if they see something worrying. They get no access and no control. Ask for
it by name.

**Voice cloning** — Software that copies a person's voice from a few seconds of
audio. Free, fast, and convincing. Defeated by a callback and a code word.

**Deepfake** — A fabricated video or image of a real person. Now used for fake
celebrity investment endorsements.

**Pig butchering** — An ugly industry term for a long-running scam that builds a
relationship for months before introducing a fake investment. The costliest
pattern in America.

**Money mule** — Someone who moves stolen money for a scammer, often without
knowing it. If a stranger asks you to receive money and forward it, or forward
packages, this is what's being asked — and it carries real legal risk for you.

**Elder fraud** — The FBI's category for fraud against people 60 and over.
**201,266 complaints and $7.7 billion reported in 2025.**

**IC3** — The FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center, at ic3.gov. Where to report.

**Cooling-off rule** — Federal law giving you three days to cancel most sales made
at your door. Door-to-door contractors are required to tell you. Many don't.

## You don't need most of this

You can forget every word on this page and still be fine. The three steps work
without any of it.

The vocabulary is here so that nobody can use jargon to make you feel out of your
depth. When someone on the phone starts using technical words, that's usually the
point — and now it won't work.
