# Help in your language

The honest summary: **almost every US federal fraud resource is published in
English and Spanish only.** For the other languages on this site, the way you get
help is not a separate phone number — it's an interpreter on the main number.

That's a real service, it's free, and most people don't know to ask for it. This
page is mostly about how to ask.

---

## The one thing to know

When someone answers, say the language in **English**, then stop talking:

> **"Urdu, please."**
> **"Farsi, please."**
> **"Amharic, please."**

You'll be put on hold briefly while an interpreter joins. Then you can speak
normally in your own language.

You do not need to explain yourself, apologize, or try to get through the call in
English first. Interpretation is a standard service these lines are built to
provide.

Each fridge sheet on this site carries that phrase in the reader's own language,
so it can be read off the refrigerator during the call.

## The two helplines

### National Elder Fraud Hotline — 833-372-8311

Run by the Department of Justice, Office for Victims of Crime. For anyone 60 or
older who has been targeted by fraud. Case managers help you report it and
connect you to other services.

Open Monday through Friday, 10 a.m. to 6 p.m. Eastern time, with services available for speakers of English, Spanish, and other languages.

Free. You can ask to remain anonymous.

### AARP Fraud Watch Helpline — 877-908-3360

Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 8 p.m. Eastern. Free for anyone, member or not.
Interpretation available. They also run free support groups for people who have
lost money.

Use this one if you want to talk something through *before* deciding whether
anything is wrong. That's what it's for.

### Eldercare Locator — 800-677-1116

Connects you to aging services in your own county, which is where
language-specific help actually lives. Interpretation available.

**This is the most useful number on this page for language access**, because
local Area Agencies on Aging often have bilingual staff or contracts with
community organizations that serve your language specifically.

## Reporting online

| Where | Languages | For |
|---|---|---|
| **reportfraud.ftc.gov** | English, Spanish | All fraud |
| **ic3.gov** | English | Anything involving internet, phone, or a wire |
| **identitytheft.gov** | English, Spanish | If personal information was taken |

The FTC's Spanish site is at **ftc.gov/es** and is a full translation, not a
summary.

**If you can't complete an online form in English**, call 833-372-8311 and ask
for an interpreter. The case manager will file the report with you over the
phone. This is a normal thing to request.

## Where local help actually comes from

National hotlines give you an interpreter. **Local organizations give you someone
who already understands the situation** — including the scams aimed specifically
at your community, which national material rarely covers.

The kinds of places worth asking, in roughly this order:

1. **Your county's Area Agency on Aging** — call 800-677-1116 to find it
2. **Mutual assistance associations and refugee resettlement agencies** — most
   metro areas have one per major community, and they usually do benefits and
   fraud help alongside everything else
3. **Your place of worship** — often the single most effective distribution point
   for this material
4. **Community health centers** with bilingual staff
5. **Ethnic media** — a community radio show or newspaper will often run this
   material for free

**We deliberately don't list specific organizations here.** They change, they
move, and a dead phone number on a page about fraud does real harm to the trust
this whole site depends on. Your Area Agency on Aging will know the current ones
for your county.

## You can do this

Asking for an interpreter is not an imposition and it is not a favor anyone is
doing you. It is a service that exists, that is paid for, and that you are
entitled to use.

Two words in English — your language, then "please" — and the rest of the
conversation happens in the language you think in. That's all it takes.
