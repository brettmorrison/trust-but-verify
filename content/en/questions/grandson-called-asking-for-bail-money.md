# My grandson called asking for bail money

**Do this first, before you read anything else on this page:**

1. **Hang up.**
2. **Call your grandson on the number you've always used** — not the number that
   called you.
3. **If he doesn't answer, call his parents right now.**

If he's fine, you have your answer. If it's real, you've lost ninety seconds and
his mother now knows, which she needed to anyway.

---

## "But they said not to tell his mother"

**That instruction is the proof.**

No lawyer, judge, or police officer has ever told a grandmother not to call her
own family. Gag orders don't work that way and don't apply to you. Court records
of an arrest are public.

The secrecy exists for one reason: the phone call you'd make would end the scam
in ten seconds. Everything else in the script is decoration around that.

---

## "But it was his voice"

It may well have sounded exactly like him. Software copies a person's voice from
a few seconds of audio — a Facebook video, a voicemail greeting, a livestream —
and it's free and takes about a minute.

You cannot beat this by listening. Nobody can. That's why the answer is to call
him back on your own number: the cloning works on the call they place, and it
cannot answer the call you place.

More: [voice cloning](/scams/voice-cloning).

## "But they knew his name"

Often they didn't. The call usually opens with "Grandma? It's me" and waits for
you to say the name. Then they use it.

And if they did know it — names, schools, and hometowns are on Facebook, in
obituaries, and in graduation announcements, or bought from a data broker.
[How they got your information](/how-they-got-your-information).

---

## Things that are never true

- **Bail is never paid in gift cards.** Not anywhere, not once.
- **No court or jail sends a courier to your home for cash.** If someone is coming
  to your door, call the police at 911.
- **Bail is not paid by wire transfer or Bitcoin** to a lawyer you've never met.
- **Real lawyers give you a firm name and a bar number** and don't mind you
  looking them up.
- **There is no two-hour deadline** after which someone spends the weekend in
  jail because a payment was late.

---

## If you can't reach anyone and you're still worried

Call the jail or the court directly — a real one, whose number you look up
yourself in the county where the arrest supposedly happened. Arrest records are
public and they can tell you in a minute whether he's there.

You can also call **833-372-8311** (National Elder Fraud Hotline, weekdays
10 a.m. to 6 p.m. Eastern) and talk to someone before you do anything.

---

## Before it happens again — set the code word

One word, shared out loud at a family meal, never posted anywhere. Then, next
time, you ask for the word.

Four minutes, and it defeats every version of this scam.

## Make the call

Whatever else is true, calling your grandson costs you ninety seconds.

If he's fine, you'll be relieved and he'll be glad you thought of him. If it's
real, you've lost nothing and gained the rest of the family. There is no version
of this where making that call was the wrong move.

---

**If money already moved:** [Do this now](/i-think-i-was-scammed). Call your bank
immediately — same-day recalls have worked. And there is no reason to be
embarrassed: this call is designed by professionals to work on people who love
their families.
