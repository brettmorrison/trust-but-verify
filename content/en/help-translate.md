# Help us check the translations

**Every page on this site that isn't in English was translated by AI, and none
of it has been validated by a native speaker yet.**

We say that on every translated page, in that language, at the top. We'd rather
lose readers than have someone trust a page we can't vouch for.

Until a page is validated, it carries a warning and it should not be printed or
handed out.

---

## What we're asking for

Not a professional translation. Not certification. Not a service.

We need **one person who speaks the language every day** to read the pages and
tell us what's wrong. That's it.

Specifically:

**What sounds translated.** Sentences that are technically correct and obviously
not written by a person. These are the ones that quietly cost you a reader's
trust.

**What sounds condescending.** This matters more than anything else on the list.
This material is read by adults in their seventies and eighties, and the fastest
way to lose them is a tone that talks down. English is careful about this. A
machine translation usually isn't, because register doesn't survive translation.

**What words your community wouldn't use.** Regional vocabulary, the wrong
formality, a term that's fine in one country and odd in another.

**What's missing.** The scams that reach your community may be different — a
remittance scam, an immigration-status threat, a scheme that only works on people
with family overseas. Tell us and we'll write the page.

**Anything factually wrong** that survived translation.

---

## What it takes

**About one to two hours.** The core page in each language is roughly 2,000
words.

**No special tools.** Read it, mark what's wrong, send us notes. Bullet points in
an email are perfect. Voice memo is fine too. Handwritten on a printout and
photographed is fine.

**You don't have to be an expert on scams.** You're checking the language, not
the security advice. If the advice sounds wrong to you, say so and we'll check
it against the federal sources — but that's a bonus, not the job.

**You don't have to fix it.** Pointing at a sentence and saying "nobody talks
like this" is complete and useful feedback. We'll rewrite it and send it back.

---

## What you get

**Your name on the page, if you want it.** Or not — plenty of people would rather
not be listed, and that's fine, no explanation needed.

**No money.** Nobody involved in this is paid, including the person who started
it. There's no budget, and there won't be one.

**The material, to use however you like.** Translate it further, put your
organization's name on it, rebrand it entirely, hand it out at your church,
mosque, temple, clinic, or community center. You don't need our permission and
you don't need to credit us.

## Who's a good fit

- Anyone bilingual who talks to older relatives in that language regularly —
  this is the best qualification there is, better than a translation degree
- Community health workers, church and mosque volunteers, senior center staff
- Adult children who already explain this stuff to their parents in two languages
- Students who need volunteer hours and speak the language at home
- Retired teachers, nurses, and social workers

If you were the person in your family who translated at the doctor's office
growing up, you are exactly who we're looking for.

---

## How to reach us

**translations [at] trustbutverifyproject [dot] org**

Tell us the language, and roughly how you use it — spoken at home, professionally,
grew up with it. That's all the screening there is.

If you'd rather look before you commit, all the files are freely downloadable and
you can read the whole thing before deciding.

---

## What happens after you send notes

1. We rewrite the pages using your notes.
2. We send them back for one more look.
3. When you say it's good, the warning banner comes off and the page is marked
   validated, with your name if you wanted it.
4. Then, and only then, it goes on refrigerators.

If a page is ever revised, the banner goes back on until someone reads it again.

---

## Why this matters more than it sounds

Somebody's grandmother is going to be standing in a kitchen with a phone against
her ear, frightened, and the only thing on the refrigerator that she can actually
read is going to be in her language or it isn't going to help her at all.

You can be the reason it's in her language. It takes an hour.
