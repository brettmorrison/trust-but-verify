Anti-slop review: the English site

An examination, not a rewrite. Nothing here has been changed or deployed.

Verdict: the site reads human. Genuinely.

Across 57 English pages the mechanical scan found 4 hits, and one of those is a false positive. No filler vocabulary anywhere. No formulaic openers, no bow-tie closers, no "whether you're X or Y", no meta-hedging, no over-signposting. Sentence rhythm is varied (median stdev 8.0). The writing has a real voice: concrete dollar figures, named cases, and claims it commits to.

The one page that reads noticeably machine-made is /protect-your-privacy, and it is the page I wrote. It has 4.9 contractions per 1,000 words against a site median of 26.7, which makes it the stiffest page on the site by a factor of five. I over-corrected away from em dashes into uniform formality.

57 English pages examined 0 filler-vocab hits 0 formulaic openers 3 em dashes (new files) 1 page reads stiff 4 safety items flagged, not touched

How this was done

The de-slop skill files were not on disk. I searched ~/Claude/outputs, ~/outputs, ./outputs and ~/.claude/skills; the only *slop* match was an unrelated GrailWatch git worktree. So I applied the ruleset as written in the request: strip em dashes, filler vocabulary, meta-hedging, rule-of-three, formulaic openers and bow-tie closers, "whether you're X or Y", over-signposting; then humanize by varying sentence length, committing to claims, preferring concrete specifics, and breaking symmetry.

Method was a mechanical pre-scan for 9 tell patterns across all English content, then reading the non-safety prose, then measuring rhythm and contraction density per page to catch what reading alone misses.

Second tell: six numbered sections all open with a bolded imperative followed by one or two sentences, with no variation. The symmetry is visible once you scroll. Breaking two or three of them, so one opens with the reason and another with a bare fact, would kill the template feel without touching what it tells people to do.

Verdict: the content is strong and specific. The voice is the problem, and it is a mechanical fix: restoring contractions alone moves it most of the way.

/aboutMostly excellent, one dense patch

Verdict first: this is the best-written page on the site. Specific and unfakeable. "$300,000 of his house, gone." A coworker's daughter sending money to a boyfriend "working in Oman." Real DOJ prosecutions with links. Nobody would mistake this for generated text.

Tell: one paragraph compresses four separate policies into telegraphic fragments, and its negation rhythm repeats a construction the home page already uses.

No shame, ever: someone who's already lost money should be able to read any page here without flinching. Free, no products, no sponsors, no affiliate links, nothing sold. No cookies, no accounts, no mailing list, see privacy for the one exception…
Split it. Give "no shame, ever" its own line, because it is the most important promise on the page and it is currently buried mid-paragraph between funding and cookies.

Minor: "hence the three steps" is the one stiff word on the page.

/ (home)Human, one duplication

Verdict: reads like a person. The anaphora is deliberate and earned, not padding: "It does not say trust nobody. It does not say the world is full of liars…"

Tell: the funding/privacy block near-duplicates the one on /about. Same negation-list construction, same content, two pages apart.

No products, no sponsors, no advertising, no mailing list, no donation button. No cookies, no accounts, nothing sold or shared.
Keep the full version on /about and cut the home page to one line that links to it. Repeating a distinctive construction verbatim across pages is the thing that reads templated, more than any single word choice.

Highest-impact fixes

  1. Restore contractions on /protect-your-privacy. Single biggest win. Takes the stiffest page on the site from 4.9 to roughly the 26.7 median and fixes the only page that actually reads generated. Mechanical, no meaning changes.
  2. Break the six-section symmetry on /protect-your-privacy. Vary how two or three sections open so the page stops looking stamped out.
  3. De-duplicate the funding/privacy block between / and /about. Full version on /about, one line and a link on home.
  4. Split the dense policy paragraph on /about so "no shame, ever" stands alone. It is the site's most important promise and it is currently buried between funding and cookies.
  5. Fix the comma splice on /printables ("in their own language, you'll need"). Leftover damage from the punctuation pass, not a writing choice.
  6. Remove the one prose em dash on /validation-status ("138 pages across 44 languages"). Table-cell dashes can stay.
  7. Remove the em dash in /help-translate line 94, or keep it deliberately and note the exception. Right now it is neither.
  8. Add the em-dash check to audit_site.py as a warning. Three have reappeared in new files since the sitewide pass, which will keep happening without a guard.
  9. Reword "hence the three steps" on /about. One stiff word on an otherwise excellent page.
