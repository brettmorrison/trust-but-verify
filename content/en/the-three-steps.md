# The three steps

Every scam in the world, however it arrives, has to get you to do one thing:
**act before you check.**

That's the trick. Not clever technology. Not a convincing accent. Speed.

So the defense isn't cleverness either. It's three steps that all do the same
thing — they put time between the request and the money.

---

## Step 1 — Look up the number yourself

**Never use the number they gave you.**

Not the number in the text. Not the number on the popup. Not the number the
caller reads out. Not the one in the email signature. Not even the number that
comes up first when you search the company's name — scammers buy those search
results too.

Use a number you already had before today:

- The back of your credit or debit card
- A paper statement
- Your bill
- The back of your Medicare or insurance card
- The number written in your own address book

If a real person from your bank called you, they will not mind you hanging up and
calling the bank. That is exactly what a bank employee expects a careful customer
to do. Say it plainly: *"I'm going to hang up and call the number on my card."*

A scammer will fight you on that sentence. **The fight is the answer.** You don't
have to win the argument. You just have to hang up.

---

## Step 2 — Call the person yourself

If the message claims to be from a person — your grandson, your daughter, your
pastor, your neighbor — call **that person**, on the number you have always used
for them.

Not the number they're texting from. People's phone numbers do not change in an
emergency.

If they don't answer, call someone else in the family. This isn't disloyalty and
it isn't going behind anyone's back. If your grandson really is in a jail cell,
his mother needs to know in the next ten minutes anyway.

A real emergency survives one more phone call. A fake one cannot.

### The family code word {#the-family-code-word}

Pick a word. A street you lived on, a dog's name, something no stranger could
guess and no computer could find. Tell it to your children, your grandchildren,
and anyone who might one day call you frightened at 2 a.m.

Then, when a frightened voice calls, you ask for the word.

This costs nothing, takes four minutes at a Sunday dinner, and defeats every
voice-cloning scam that exists today. Software can copy your grandson's voice
from a three-second video. It cannot copy something that was never said out loud
on the internet.

---

## Step 3 — Wait a day

This is the step people skip, and the one that works even when the other two fail.

Nothing legitimate in your life requires money to move in the next twenty
minutes. Not the IRS. Not Medicare. Not your bank's fraud department. Not a
utility about to shut off your power. Not a warrant. Not a lawsuit. Not a
sweepstakes deadline. Not an investment window.

Every one of those has a mailing address, an office, a process, and a paper
trail. Every one of them will still exist tomorrow morning.

**Urgency is not evidence that something is real. Urgency is evidence that
someone needs you to stop thinking.**

If a full day is too long, take twenty minutes. Make coffee. Tell one person out
loud what's being asked of you. Saying it out loud to another human being is
remarkably effective — most people hear the problem in their own sentence before
the other person even answers.

---

## What to say

You don't owe anyone a conversation. But if it helps to have a line ready:

> "I don't discuss money on calls I didn't start. I'm going to hang up and call
> the number on my statement."

> "I have a rule that I wait a day on anything financial. If this is real, it'll
> still be real tomorrow."

> "Let me check with my daughter first."

Or nothing at all. Hanging up is not rude. It is a complete answer.

## If you remember one thing

**The number they gave you is the scam.**

Almost every scheme on this site collapses the moment you dial a number they
didn't choose for you.

## You already know how to do this

None of these three steps is a skill. You already know how to hang up a phone,
look at the back of a card, and sleep on a decision. You've been doing all three
your whole life.

The only new part is doing them **before** the money moves instead of after. That
one change puts you ahead of every scammer working today — and they know it,
which is exactly why they spend so much energy trying to keep you on the line.
