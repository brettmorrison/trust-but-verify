# How they ask to be paid

You may not be able to tell whether a caller really works for your bank.

But you can always tell how they want the money. And the payment method gives it
away faster and more reliably than anything else in the conversation.

**The rule:** scammers only accept payments that can't be reversed. That's the
entire logic. So a request for an irreversible payment, from someone who called
you, is the tell.

---

## The six that should stop you cold

### 1. Gift cards

Buy cards, scratch off the back, read the numbers over the phone.

**Nobody legitimate has ever asked for this.** Not the IRS, not Apple, not
Microsoft, not your electric company, not a court, not a bail bondsman, not a
sheriff's deputy, not a hospital.

If gift cards come up in any conversation about a debt, a fine, a fee, a bail, a
refund, or a computer problem. That is the end of the analysis. There is no
second interpretation.

### 2. Wire transfer

Western Union, MoneyGram, or a wire from your bank. Money sent this way is
usually gone within minutes and almost never recoverable.

A wire to someone you have not met, in a hurry, is the scam's favorite ending.

### 3. Cryptocurrency and Bitcoin ATMs

Those machines in gas stations and convenience stores. Somebody guiding you
through one over the phone is stealing from you. There is no other reason for
that phone call to exist.

Legitimate US government agencies do not accept cryptocurrency. Your bank's fraud
department does not ask you to convert your savings to Bitcoin to protect it.
That sentence should sound as strange as it is.

### 4. Cash by mail or courier

Cash in a magazine, cash in a box, cash handed to a young man who comes to your
door to "collect it for the investigation." Gold and silver bars too, that
version is real and growing.

No agency on earth sends someone to your home to collect valuables. Not one.

### 5. Payment apps to a stranger

Zelle, Venmo, Cash App, Apple Pay. These are wonderful for splitting a dinner
with someone you know. They are designed for people who already trust each
other, and they move like cash, instantly, and usually with no way back.

**Zelle in particular:** most banks treat a Zelle payment you authorized as
final, even if you were tricked into it. Assume it's gone the moment you press
send.

### 6. Buying your own "safe account"

Being told to move your money into a new account "for safekeeping while we
investigate."

This is the ending of the [Phantom Hacker scheme](/scams/phantom-hacker), and it
is the one that empties whole retirement accounts. Your bank will never ask you to
move money out of your bank. There is no such thing as a government safe account.

---

### The reverse: money coming *to* you

One more pattern, because it runs backwards and slips past everything above.

If someone sends you a check and asks you to forward part of it onward, that
check is fake, and you will owe your bank the full amount. Your bank makes the
funds available in days; discovering the check is fake takes weeks. "It cleared"
only means the bank fronted you the money and can take it back.

This shows up most often as a job offer. See
[the job that doesn't exist](/scams/job-scams).

## What real payments look like

| Legitimate | Scam-preferred |
|---|---|
| A bill in the mail with an account number | Gift cards |
| Check or automatic payment | Wire to a person |
| Credit card (which you can dispute) | Bitcoin ATM |
| Paying on a website *you* navigated to | Cash by courier |
| A payment plan and a written notice | Payment app to a stranger |

Credit cards are worth naming specifically. They have the strongest fraud
protections of any payment method available to an ordinary person. If you must
pay somebody you're unsure of, a credit card is the least dangerous way to be
wrong.

---

## The one sentence

> **"How do you want to be paid?"**

Ask early. Ask before you agree to anything. It's a completely normal question,
you can ask it without accusing anyone, and the answer often ends the
conversation for you.

## One question, and you're ahead

You don't have to out-argue anyone or prove they're lying. You just have to know
what real money movement looks like, and now you do.

Ask how they want to be paid, listen to the answer, and you'll often know
everything you need to know before they finish the sentence.
