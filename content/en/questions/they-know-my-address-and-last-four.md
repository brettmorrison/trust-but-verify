# It's a scam if they already know my address?

**Yes — knowing things about you proves nothing.**

This trips up careful people more than almost anything else, because it feels
like proof. It isn't. It's the cheapest part of the whole operation.

---

## What a caller can easily know about you

Legally, and for a few dollars:

- Your full name and how you spell it
- Your current address and every address you've lived at
- Your age and date of birth
- Your phone numbers, including old ones
- Your relatives' names and ages
- What your house is worth and whether you own it
- Your bank's name
- The last four digits of a card
- What you've bought and roughly what you earn
- That your spouse died last year

All of it comes from data brokers, breached databases, public records, and
obituaries. See [how did they get my information](/questions/how-did-they-get-my-information).

---

## Why the last four digits feel so convincing

Because you were taught it identifies you. It's what a company asks *you* for.

But that's backwards. It works as a check when **you** call **them** — you're
proving who you are to an institution you chose to contact.

It proves nothing in the other direction. Card numbers appear in breaches
constantly, and the last four are printed on every receipt you've ever thrown
away.

**Something being used to identify you is not the same as something identifying
them.**

---

## The rule that handles all of it

Nothing a caller *tells* you can verify a caller. Not details about you, not
caller ID, not a badge number, not a case number, not a supervisor put on the
line, not a callback number they provide, not an official-looking email or letter
they send you.

All of it can be manufactured, and all of it is, daily.

**The only verification that works is the call you place, to a number you looked
up.**

That's it. That's the entire test. And its beauty is that you never have to
evaluate their evidence at all.

---

## What to say

> "I understand. I'm going to hang up and call the number on my statement, and
> we can continue there."

A real employee says "of course." A scammer explains why you can't, or why that
department won't know, or why the line is compromised, or why there's no time.

**That explanation is your answer.** You don't have to argue with it. Just hang
up.

## Why this is good news

If details about you were proof, you'd have no way to tell anyone apart. But
details are cheap and the callback is free, so the test that matters is one you
can always run.

Let them recite whatever they've bought. You still get to hang up and dial a
number of your own choosing, and that's the only evidence that has ever
counted.
