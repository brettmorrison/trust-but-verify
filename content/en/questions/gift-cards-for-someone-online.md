# Should I buy gift cards for someone I met online?

**No.**

This is the clearest answer on this entire site. There is no version of this that
turns out well, and there is no exception.

---

## Why it's this absolute

Gift cards are the scammer's favorite payment because they behave like cash that
can be spent from another country in minutes. Once the numbers on the back are
read out loud, the money is gone, and there is no bank, no chargeback, and no
recall.

That's why they ask for them. It's the only reason anyone would.

**Nobody legitimate has ever needed to be paid in gift cards.** Not the IRS, not
Microsoft, not Apple, not a utility, not a court, not a bail bondsman, not a
hospital, not a soldier, not a surgeon, not a customs office, not a friend
overseas.

---

## The stories you'll hear

Every one of these is a scam, without exception:

- He's deployed and can't access his accounts
- She's a doctor overseas and her cards are blocked
- It's for a customs fee on a package coming to you
- It's the fastest way to send help
- He'll pay you back double when the contract closes
- The IRS/court/police will accept them as payment
- It's to verify your account
- It's for a computer repair fee
- Your grandson's bail

---

## If you're being asked right now

**Send nothing. Not one card. Not a small one to help out.**

The small first request is a test, of whether you'll say yes, and of whether
you'll tell anybody. Passing it doesn't end the asking. It begins it.

Then, before you do anything else:

**Tell one person out loud.** A friend, a son or daughter, a neighbor, the front
desk. Not to ask permission, just say the situation out loud to another human
being. Most people hear the answer in their own sentence.

If you'd rather talk to a stranger who won't judge you and doesn't know your
family: **877-908-3360**, the AARP Fraud Watch Helpline, weekdays 8 a.m. to
8 p.m. Eastern. Free for anyone. They handle exactly this every single day.

---

## If someone at the store asks you about it

Many stores now train cashiers to ask when someone buys several gift cards, and
they get told to mind their own business fairly often.

**Tell them the truth.** They aren't being nosy. That question exists because it
has stopped thousands of losses, and the cashier standing in front of you may be
the last person able to help before the money is gone.

If someone on the phone has told you what to say to the cashier, that alone
settles it. You are being coached to defeat a protection that exists for you.

---

## If you already sent the numbers

Move fast, occasionally the money is still on the card:

1. Call the **card company**, not the store: Apple, Google Play, Target, Amazon,
   eBay, Steam. Say the card was used in a scam.
2. Have the **card numbers and the receipt** ready. Keep the physical cards.
3. Report at **reportfraud.ftc.gov** and **ic3.gov**.
4. Expect a "we can recover it for you" call in the coming weeks. That's the
   [next scam](/scams/recovery-scam).

Full list: [I think I was scammed](/i-think-i-was-scammed).

## The easiest "no" you'll ever say

Almost nothing in life is this clear-cut. This is: gift cards to someone you
haven't met, for any reason at all, is always a scam.

You never have to weigh it, research it, or feel bad about declining. One rule,
zero exceptions, and you're done.
